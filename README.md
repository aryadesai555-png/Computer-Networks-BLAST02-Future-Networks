# Computer Networks: The Future of Networking

An exploration of how artificial intelligence, quantum technologies, blockchain, post-quantum cryptography, and 6G may shape the future of computer networks.

## Overview

Computer networks are evolving to support increasing traffic, connected devices, stronger security, and more demanding applications. This project explores how emerging technologies could extend today's Internet and contribute to a possible network architecture for 2035.

The project combines networking fundamentals, a practical network experiment, technology research, and a proposed future-network architecture.

## Research Question

**How could AI, quantum networking, post-quantum cryptography, blockchain, and 6G transform computer networks by 2035?**

## Technologies Explored

- **AI-native networking:** Using machine learning to predict congestion, detect faults, identify unusual traffic, and support network optimization.
- **Quantum networking:** Exploring communication of quantum information and the challenges of building quantum networks.
- **Post-quantum cryptography (PQC):** Examining cryptographic methods designed to resist attacks from quantum computers.
- **Blockchain:** Investigating shared records, device identity, access control, and trust in distributed environments.
- **6G:** Exploring the goals, potential applications, and limitations of the next generation of mobile networking.

## Network Experiment

A basic connectivity experiment was performed on macOS using the following command:

```bash
ping -c 4 google.com
```

### Results

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---:|
| Packets transmitted | 4 |
| Packets received | 4 |
| Packet loss | 0% |
| Minimum round-trip time | 19.536 ms |
| Average round-trip time | 37.538 ms |
| Maximum round-trip time | 76.287 ms |

The four probes received replies, with an average round-trip time of 37.538 milliseconds. The response times varied, but this small experiment cannot identify the exact cause of the variation or establish long-term network performance.

## Proposed Network of 2035

The proposed architecture explores how existing Internet infrastructure could work alongside emerging technologies.

Its main components include:

- End-user devices and Internet of Things (IoT) devices
- Access technologies such as Wi-Fi, 5G, 6G, and fiber
- Routers and conventional IP networking using TCP and UDP
- Edge computing and cloud services
- AI-assisted network monitoring and control
- Cybersecurity, encryption, authentication, and post-quantum cryptography
- Blockchain-based shared records where appropriate
- A specialized quantum communication pathway for potential future applications

The architecture is conceptual. It does not assume that every technology will be deployed everywhere or that quantum networking will replace conventional Internet traffic.

## Key Findings

- AI could have a broad impact by helping operators monitor traffic, predict congestion, and manage network resources.
- Post-quantum cryptography addresses security risks to classical communication and requires careful migration planning.
- Quantum networking may support specialized applications, but practical implementation faces significant technical challenges.
- Blockchain can support shared records in suitable environments, although conventional databases may be more efficient in other situations.
- 6G may extend mobile-network capabilities, but its potential benefits must be evaluated alongside cost, coverage, energy use, and deployment challenges.
- The most likely future is an evolution of today's Internet rather than a complete replacement of its core networking principles.

## Limitations

The ping experiment represents only a small sample of network behavior. The proposed Network of 2035 is a conceptual architecture rather than a deployed implementation. The benefits of emerging technologies depend on technical progress, cost, interoperability, security, and real-world testing.
